About This Site

This explorer accompanies Alina Scott’s dissertation, Murder by Inches: Family, Race, and Wampanoag Networks of Petitioners (1800–1870) (PhD diss., University of Texas at Austin, 2025). It turns the dissertation’s dated actions, recurring people, places and arguments into views you can filter and follow, and pairs them with one of its central sources, the 1834 petition of the Mashpee Indians, which gives the dissertation and this site their title.

How the events were gathered

  • Every dated action was extracted from the dissertation’s full text: the front-matter timeline, each chapter and the conclusion. Duplicates were merged.
  • Each event belongs to the part of the dissertation it comes from (Preface and Introduction, the four “spaces of protest,” and the Conclusion), carries one primary theme, and may carry secondary themes added on a later review of the manuscript.
  • Dates follow the source: approximate dates keep their “c.,” and events that span years are drawn as faint bars from start to end. Of – dated events plus – undated, only dated events appear on the timeline.
  • The timeline’s axis is stretched on purpose: each 25-year stretch gets width according to how many events fall in it, so busy decades are readable. Long empty stretches are compressed and hatched.

Recurring figures

The – recurring figures were identified from a curated list of names and their variants, then traced through every event to count appearances and see who appears alongside whom.

Map locations

Places are matched automatically from the place names written in each event’s text, then placed using a list of locations. Some markers are approximate, some stand for a whole state or the federal government rather than one point, and – events name no place that could be mapped. Treat the map as a guide to where activity concentrates, not as exact locations.

Chapter Synthesis

The synthesis is drawn from the dissertation’s research argument map: each section’s central argument, its evidence, key people, places and themes, and a one-sentence claim.

The 1834 petition and its signers

  • The text and the four lists of signers come from the Native Northeast Portal’s annotated transcription of the “Petition of the Mashpee Indians to the Senate and House of Representatives of the Commonwealth of Massachusetts.” Spelling and wording follow that transcription. The footnotes are the Portal’s editorial notes.
  • Signers were compared by hand with the people in the dissertation. Of – transcribed names, – are linked to dissertation events.
  • In the dissertation means the same name appears there, or a documented spelling of it (William Apes is spelled “Apess” in the dissertation).
  • Possible match (– names) means it is likely, but not certain, that the signer is the person in the dissertation: a middle initial differs, a married name may differ, or the dissertation’s record comes from another time or community. Each possible match carries a note explaining why.
  • An unlinked name may still be in the dissertation under a spelling or name not yet matched.
